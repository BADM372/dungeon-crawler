Not a single sound file

Most games record sounds with a microphone and play them back. Cube Crawler has no sound files at all.

Every swoosh, thump, coin, footstep, monster growl and splash is calculated, right when you hear it, by your browser's built-in synthesizer.

A sound is a wave: air pushed back and forth, very fast. The game describes each sound as a recipe: which waves to mix, how they change, how loud they are at each moment.

Press the buttons and watch the wave. Change the pitch: the same recipe can make a small spider's hit or a big Stunder's thud.

The ingredients of a sound

  • Tones: a smooth wave that repeats. How often it repeats per second is the pitch (in Hertz, Hz). The shape matters too: a sine sounds soft and round, a square buzzy, a sawtooth harsh.
  • Slides: a tone that falls from high to low sounds like a thump or a "pew". Rising sounds like magic.
  • Noise: random numbers! It sounds like hissing, wind, or rushing water.
  • Filters change noise: a low-pass filter keeps the low part (muffled, like through a wall), a high-pass keeps the high part (thin, crackly).
  • Envelopes: how the volume changes. Fast up and fast down is a click. Slow down is a ring.

Clever details

  • Coins climb: pick up coins quickly one after another, and each one plays a little higher. It feels like a jackpot!
  • Left and right: a monster on the left side of the screen sounds from your left speaker.
  • Footsteps know what you walk on: grass, gravel, snow, ice, mud, stone, wood, rope bridges, iron. They are prepared in a few slightly different versions, so they never sound like a machine.
  • Monsters have voices: a sound when they notice you, one when they wind up an attack, and one when they die.
  • The background: water, lava bubbling, wind under the open sky, drips in caves, louder the closer you are.

Music written as text

The music is not recorded either. Each of the 13 songs is a text file in the music/ folder, written in a format called Scrimshaw. A tiny synthesizer in the browser plays it.

Each line belongs to one instrument (a voice). Notes are letters and numbers: G5 is the note G, high up. Dashes make a note last longer, a dot is a rest, | separates bars.

Every depth has its own song: a waltz for the town, a marimba tune for the meadows, an oboe for the snowy peaks, taiko drums for the volcanic depths, a choir for the abyss.

All songs share a little tune, the game's motif: the notes 1 2 3 5 4 of their scale. Listen for it!

Try it: music that feels the danger

This is the game's real music player. Press Play, then switch the mood. Each mood picks which voices play: when danger comes, the tune drops out and drums take over; in a fight a horn joins in; for a boss, a choir and a gong.

Orange voices are playing. In the game, you never press these buttons: the game picks the mood by looking at the monsters around you.

How the game picks a mood

MOODWHEN
explorenothing going on
treasurea closed chest within 6 cells
sensea monster within 8 cells, not after you
risea monster chasing you
fighta monster after you within 4 cells, or blows in the last 6 seconds
bossa guardian after you
  1. Every frame, find the most exciting mood that fits.
  2. More exciting than now? Switch at once: danger should be heard right away.
  3. Calmer? Only switch after the calm has lasted a moment (1 to 5 seconds). Otherwise the music would jump around in a fight.
  4. New voices come in on the next beat-step, and pick up notes that are already playing. Leaving voices fade out gently.

The result: you can often hear a monster before you see it.

The real code: the coin sound

// building blocks: a gain envelope, noise and pitch-swept oscillators
function amp(t, attack, peak, decay, out) {
  const g = ctx.createGain();
  g.gain.setValueAtTime(0.0001, t);
  g.gain.exponentialRampToValueAtTime(peak, t + attack);
  g.gain.exponentialRampToValueAtTime(0.0001, t + attack + decay);
  g.connect(out);
  return g;
}

coin(t, out, p) {    // two quick bright notes; rapid pickups climb in pitch
  tone('triangle', 1568 * p, 1568 * p, t, 0.08, amp(t, 0.002, 0.32, 0.07, out));
  tone('triangle', 2093 * p, 2093 * p, t + 0.055, 0.2, amp(t + 0.055, 0.002, 0.3, 0.18, out));
},

// in sfx(): quick coins raise the pitch, up to 10 steps
if (name === 'coin') {
  combo = now - comboAt < 0.4 ? combo + 1 : 0; comboAt = now;
  pitch *= 1 + Math.min(combo, 10) * 0.035;
}

amp makes an envelope: start silent, rise to peak in attack seconds, fade away in decay seconds.

The coin is two notes: 1568 Hz (a high G), then 55 milliseconds later 2093 Hz (a high C). Each one a triangle wave, each one with its own envelope.

p is the pitch multiplier. The combo counter adds 3.5% for every coin picked up within 0.4 seconds of the last one.

The real code: rising fast, calming slowly

const HOLD = { treasure: 1, sense: 2, rise: 3, fight: 4, boss: 5 };   // s the calmer reason must hold

export function updateMusic(dt) {
  if (!state.map) return;
  playSong(songFor(state.map));
  const want = forced ?? wanted();
  if (forced || RANK[want] >= RANK[mood]) { mood = want; calm = 0; }
  else if ((calm += dt) > HOLD[mood]) { mood = want; calm = 0; }
  setMood(mood);
}

export const songFor = map => map.enemyFree ? 'town'
  : map.depth >= MAX_DEPTH ? 'core' : themeAt(map.depth).music;

RANK orders the moods from calm (explore) to fierce (boss).

If the wanted mood is at least as fierce, switch now. Otherwise, count up calm; only when it is longer than the current mood's hold time, give in.

songFor: the town song where there are no enemies (the town and the merchant camp), the core song at the very bottom, and otherwise the song named by the depth's theme.
